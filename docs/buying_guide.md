# First-Time Property Buying Guide

## Set a realistic budget
Plan for the purchase price plus closing costs, which typically add 2% to 5% of the price (registration, taxes, agent fees, legal fees). Keep an emergency fund separate from the down payment. A common rule is that total housing costs should stay under 30% of monthly income.

## Down payment and financing
Most lenders expect a down payment of 10% to 20%. A larger down payment lowers monthly payments and total interest. Compare at least three lenders and check whether the rate is fixed or variable, whether early repayment carries a penalty, and the total cost over the loan term, not just the monthly payment.

## Inspecting a property
Visit at different times of day. Check water pressure, damp patches, cracks in walls, electrical wiring, roof condition, and the quality of fittings. For older properties, hire a qualified inspector. Ask about utility bills, maintenance fees, and any pending repairs.

## Negotiation
Research recent sale prices for similar properties nearby. Point out defects with evidence. Never negotiate without knowing your maximum price, and be prepared to walk away.
